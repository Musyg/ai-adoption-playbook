# Public-service track — formal gates

## Principle

Efficiency gains alone do not legitimize a public system. Legal mandate, proportionality, fundamental rights, transparency, accessibility, recourse, and archiving are part of the outcome.

The [Mont-Rive City synthetic case](../../examples/en/public-sector-planning-dossier-business-agent.md) applies these gates to an A2 administrative agent that checks planning dossiers. It preserves the counter and postal channel, makes the low case economically non-viable, and keeps 100% of public decisions with qualified staff.

## Gate P0 — Public opportunity

Require:

- the public-service problem and population concerned;
- the authority’s mandate and competence;
- consideration of non-AI options;
- a baseline for quality, time, cost, access, and errors;
- an administrative owner and decision authority.

## Gate P1 — Legality and impacts

- qualify purpose, data, decisions, and affected people;
- determine the impact assessments required;
- assess non-discrimination, accessibility, language, and digital exclusion;
- define notice, explanation, challenge, and human recourse;
- involve data protection, legal, security, archives, and the relevant service.

## Gate P2 — Procurement and architecture

- auditable requirements in the procurement and contract;
- access to logs, documentation, and evidence;
- subprocessors, regions, transfers, retention, and training;
- notification of model changes and incidents;
- ownership, export, deletion, continuity, and exit;
- prohibition on supplier secrecy preventing lawful oversight;
- accessibility and interoperability tests.

The European model contractual clauses for public procurement of AI systems are a guidance resource and must be adapted to applicable law.

## Gate P3 — Independent evaluation

- a test set representative of the population and rare cases;
- results by segment and severity;
- tests for security, abuse, drift, and unavailability;
- independent review of thresholds and evidence;
- documented limits and uncertainties;
- no authority to act beyond what was tested.

## Gate P4 — Controlled pilot

- shadow mode before any influence;
- bounded population, duration, data, and authorized staff;
- human oversight with the necessary time and authority;
- complaint channel and immediate stop mechanism;
- public or internal comparison with the baseline, as obligations require.

## Gate P5 — Production and accountability

- a formal decision and acceptance of residual risks by the competent authority;
- system registration and appropriate notice;
- logging, archiving, monitoring, and audits;
- operational human recourse;
- service continuity without AI;
- reassessment after a significant change;
- a retirement date and conditions.

## Stop conditions

Stop or return to the safe process in the event of an unauthorized legal effect, severe discrimination, a data breach, loss of traceability, unavailable recourse, an unevaluated supplier change, or authority exceeding the granted powers.
